Two-Tier Airlift Tower — design study

Six hydroponic succulents on two levels, water lifted from one shared reservoir by an uplift tube. The air pump stays outside the glass; only a hose comes in, plugged into a barb on the tube foot.     How the airlift is arranged. Straight from the reference sketch: the pump lives outside the glass, a single air hose comes over the rim and plugs into a barb on the side of the tube foot, and the foot itself is a funnel that draws water in off the bottom. Air injected at the foot rises inside the tube and drags water with it. Nothing electrical goes in the water and there is no air stone loose in the reservoir.

    The 70 % rule is the real ceiling. An uplift tube only works while most of its length is under water — the sketch marks it “ống phải ngập >70%”. The reservoir is only ~74 mm deep, so the whole tube can be about 100 mm long at most, which puts a hard ceiling on the discharge height no pump wattage can move. That is why a 5 W pump and a 1 W pump reach the same height; the bigger one only bubbles harder.

    Why the tiers are lobed instead of round. A round disc has to be wide enough to hold the planting holes, and then everything between the holes and the rim is dead material that also shades whatever is underneath. Following the outline of the holes instead gives a peanut on top and a clover below, turned 90° to each other, so the upper plants sit over the lower plate’s gaps and light reaches down. It also deletes the leftover wall slivers between each hole and the rim.

    Water gets down by overflowing, not by draining. Each basin has notches cut into its inner wall with their sills exactly at the working water depth. Water above that level spills over, runs down the outside of the tube and lands in the basin below — and out of the lower basin the same way, into the glass. Running the water down a surface rather than dropping it through air is what keeps it from splashing. Holes in the floor would drain the basin dry instead of holding a depth.

    The glass profile is interpolated, not measured. Inner mouth Ø90 and 80 mm from the base to the low side of the slanted rim are real measurements; the bulge of the body and the curve of the base are guesses from a photograph.
