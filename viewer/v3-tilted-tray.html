Sunken Basin — design study

Six succulents plus a climber, in one specific glass. The basin hangs below the reservoir surface and fills by gravity, so the roots sit in water and nothing is ever poured over the leaves. No printable files exist yet. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, hold Shift to pan.

    Nothing is poured from above. That was the mistake in the last version: water falling into the wells would land on the leaves, and a wet succulent crown rots. Here the basin floor sits below the water line in the glass and is open to it through ports, so the two levels simply equalise. Fill the glass and the basin fills itself. Stop the pump, unplug it, forget about it — the water stays exactly where it is, because it is the same body of water as the reservoir.

    The root shelf is level even though the tray leans. Each well has a ring ledge at one common height. The root ball sits on that ledge with the crown well clear of the water, and the roots hang through the opening into it. Because the ledge height is shared, every plant meets the water at the same depth — the lean of the tray does not change how wet any particular plant gets.

    The air pump is only an air pump. Its tube ends under the water surface, so it bubbles and stirs without throwing a single droplet. It is there for dissolved oxygen at the roots, not to move water anywhere. That also retires the whole 70 % submergence problem — the outlet is submerged by definition.

    Why this file is not a configurator. It is dimensioned for one measured tumbler: inner mouth Ø90, rim leaning 21.3° over a 35 mm rise, 80 mm from the base to the low side of the rim and 115 mm to the high side. A second glass will get a second file rather than another row of sliders.
