Tilted Tray, Level Pots — design study

One tier, six plants. The tray leans on the glass's slanted rim; every pot stays level and holds a reserve of water, so switching the pump off does not leave the roots dry. No printable files exist yet. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, hold Shift to pan.

    Why the pots are separate parts. A tray that leans 21.3° but carries level pots cannot be printed in one piece: whichever way you lay it down, either the tray underside or the pot floors end up as a shallow ramp, and a shallow ramp is a severe overhang. Splitting solves it completely — the tray prints flat in its own plane with the pot seats as tilted countersinks opening upwards, and each pot prints upright on its own flat bottom. No supports anywhere, and the pots lift out for cleaning. This is the same trick V1 used: splitting a part unlocks shapes.

    Why each pot keeps water. The outlet from each pot is a notch part-way up its wall, not a hole in its floor. Below the notch sill the water has nowhere to go, so turning the pump off leaves that depth standing in every pot. A hole in the floor would drain all six back into the glass and leave the roots in air.

    The tilt is the plumbing. Because the tray leans, the six pots sit at six different heights. Water fed to the highest one fills it to its sill, spills onto the tray surface, runs downhill into the next pot's inlet notch, and so on down to the lowest pot, which spills off the low edge of the tray back into the glass. No channels, no pipes between pots — the slope does the work. At zero tilt there is no cascade at all, which is why the tilt slider has a floor.

    The glass, measured. Inner mouth Ø90 across and 96.6 mm along the slope, rim leaning 21.3° with a 35 mm rise, 80 mm from the base to the low side of the rim and 115 mm to the high side. Those are real measurements. The bulge of the body and the curve of the base are interpolated from a photograph.

    The 70 % rule still applies. An uplift tube needs most of its length under water, so with a 74 mm reservoir it cannot discharge much above 104 mm. A tray leaning the full 21.3° puts its top pot near 115 mm, out of reach — the checks say which pots the airlift can actually feed. Reduce the tilt, or use a small 5 V submersible pump.
